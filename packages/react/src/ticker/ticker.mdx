# Ticker

One line showing the newest of a sequence, such as the step an agent is working on. Items sit on a drum like iOS's picker: a new item rolls in, and the reader can roll back through earlier ones. To show every step at once, use [Timeline](/components/timeline/).

## Usage

```tsx
import {Ticker} from '@earendil-works/elanor-react';

<Ticker.Root active>
  {steps.map((step) => (
    <Ticker.Item key={step.id} label={step.text}>
      {step.icon}
      <span>{step.text}</span>
    </Ticker.Item>
  ))}
</Ticker.Root>;
```

Items are listed oldest first, with stable keys; the last is the newest. Each is `semantic.dimension.size.small` (24px) tall and lays out its icon and text in a row, `semantic.dimension.space.gap.small` (8px) apart; text in an element, such as a `span`, ends in an ellipsis. The ticker fills its row's height, at least three items (72px); items further than three and a half from the middle are not rendered, and the others fade with their distance.

The item facing the reader inherits the ticker's color, `semantic.color.text.primary` in most contexts; the others are `semantic.color.text.secondary` with `semantic.color.icon.secondary` icons. Set `color` on the ticker (with `className`) or an ancestor to recolor the current item, such as a finished summary in `semantic.color.text.secondary`.

Screen readers hear the newest item's `label` politely; the drawn items besides the current one are hidden from them.

## Scrolling

The reader can drag the drum, or turn it with the mouse wheel. The physics follow iOS's picker: the drum moves one to one under the finger once it has moved 6px; on release, the speed over the last 80ms is projected to where iOS's deceleration would stop it, and the drum decays to the nearest item at that rate. A slow release or a short move snaps to the nearest item. Past the ends it gives like a rubber band, and catching it stops it. The mouse wheel turns it like a scroll area nested in the page: until it reaches its end in that direction, then the page scrolls.

The ticker follows the newest item. While the reader has scrolled within the last four seconds, new items wait; then it rolls all the way to the newest. More than two items arriving at once, such as when a sequence loads, jump to the newest without rolling. The roll is driven by animation frames, so it does not stop when the reader prefers reduced motion; the reader controls it.

## Activate

With `onActivate`, the ticker is a button named by the label of the item facing the reader. A tap, not a drag, calls it with the key of the item the reader rolled to, or `undefined` on the newest item, for example to open that step's details. The arrow keys move one item. The ticker stops its taps' clicks from reaching the elements around it, so a row around it can open the same thing for clicks beside it. Without `onActivate`, the ticker scrolls but is not focusable.

## Active

`active` makes the newest item breathe with `semantic.transition.pulse` while it faces the reader, to show that work is going on. It stops when the reader prefers reduced motion.

## Negative

`negative` colors an item `semantic.color.text.negative` and its icons `semantic.color.icon.negative`, such as a failed step. It stays red on the drum.

## Settled

`settled` shows only the item facing the reader, without its neighbours, such as a summary once the work is done.
