# Drawer

A modal panel that slides in from a screen edge, such as a bottom sheet or mobile navigation. It traps focus, locks page scrolling, and can be swiped away.

<Demo example="./examples/demo.tsx" />

## Usage

```tsx
import {Drawer} from '@earendil-works/elanor-react';

<Drawer.Root>
  <Drawer.Trigger />
  <Drawer.Portal>
    <Drawer.Backdrop />
    <Drawer.Viewport>
      <Drawer.Popup>
        <Drawer.Content>
          <Drawer.Title />
          <Drawer.Description />
          <Drawer.Close />
        </Drawer.Content>
      </Drawer.Popup>
    </Drawer.Viewport>
  </Drawer.Portal>
</Drawer.Root>;
```

A bottom sheet shows a grip at its top edge, a cue that it can be dragged away; side panels have none. Popup scrolls within the viewport. Content keeps text selectable with a mouse; add `data-base-ui-swipe-ignore` to controls that handle their own gestures.

## Drag to close

A bottom sheet follows the finger through a rubber band: light at first, heavier the further it goes. It closes past a third of its height or on a clear flick, and otherwise springs back; pulled up, it gives only a little. A drag starts where the content under the finger is scrolled to its top. Elsewhere the content scrolls, and once it reaches the top the same finger takes over the sheet. Sheets with snap points and side panels keep Base UI's swipe.

## Side panel

`swipeDirection="left"` or `"right"` turns the drawer into a side panel; the default, `"down"`, is a bottom sheet. Directions are physical, so they don't flip in right-to-left layouts.

Close the drawer when the user navigates, with `actionsRef.current.close()` or controlled `open`. Don't hide an open drawer with CSS at a breakpoint; close it instead. Keep its `open` state separate from [Sidebar](/components/sidebar/)'s `collapsed`.

## Pages

Put a `Drawer.Stack` directly in the Popup for a stack of pages, like a phone's navigation stack. Its children are `Drawer.Page` elements with keys, bottom first; the last is shown. Each page is a card with its own scroll position and a sticky header: a back button on deeper pages, the title on one line, and a close button on the first page, with 16px (`semantic.dimension.space.padding.xLarge`) below it before the content. The first page's title is the drawer's `Drawer.Title`; deeper titles are `h3`. Add a page to push it and remove the last one in `onBack` to go back; `backLabel` and `closeLabel` name the buttons.

A pushed page slides in from the inline end over the one below, which shifts aside and dims, with `semantic.transition.movement.drawer`; going back reverses it, and the popped page stays rendered until it has left. A swipe towards the inline end goes back, past 40% of the width or on a flick; content that scrolls sideways keeps its own swipes. A bottom sheet with a stack is 85% of the viewport's height with rounded top corners; a side panel keeps its full height and rounds its inner edge. When content arrives at the end of a page, change its `follow` prop (such as a count): if the reader is at the end, the page scrolls to the new end.

### Fade

`transition="fade"` crossfades the pages with `semantic.transition.fade` instead of sliding them: a pushed page fades in over the one below, and a popped page fades out. Pages don't move and there is no swipe back. Use it where sliding pages would look out of place, such as a panel docked beside a desktop layout.

## Nested sheets

Nest a Drawer inside a Popup to stack bottom sheets. With `data-stacked` on each Popup, parent sheets shrink behind the open sheet. Closing a nested sheet returns focus to its trigger.

## Overlay placement

The application decides how overlays stack. With an elevated header, give `Drawer.Portal` `position: fixed`, `inset: 0`, and `z-index: var(--semantic-number-layer-overlay)`. Leave the backdrop, viewport, and nested popups on their default layers.
